04 / EXPIRING REFUND CHECK

Preserve the dispute when depositing.

Time sensitive. Dmitri says the $1,655.06 check expires in about 10 days and carries “payment in full” / release language. The exact placement and wording remain to be inspected. Photograph the check front and back and any enclosure before writing on it.
  1. On the back, before deposit, write “Without prejudice and under protest; all rights reserved. Not accepted as full settlement or release.” Then sign. “Contested” alone is less explicit than the language recognized by New York courts.
  2. Send a brief reply in the existing Clara thread before or when depositing: “I am depositing the $1,655.06 partial security-deposit refund under protest and without prejudice. I reject any payment-in-full or release condition and reserve all claims for the remaining deposit and rent abatement.” Save the sent message.
  3. Deposit before the printed expiry date, preferably with a teller if the endorsement is crowded. Keep images of the final endorsement, the bank receipt, and later clearing confirmation. If the bank rejects it, keep the notation and get the reason in writing if possible.

This is a risk-reducing record, not a guarantee. Huimin Sun v Cai recognized “without prejudice and under protest” on a check as a reservation of rights. Simone v Beach Realty allowed a tenant’s deposit claim despite cashing a partial refund accompanied by full-payment/release language; Guadagni v Chong barred a deposit claim after cashing a final-settlement check. Barnes v Mruvka held that an objection sent days after an unrestricted deposit was too late. The current statute is UCC § 1-308.
