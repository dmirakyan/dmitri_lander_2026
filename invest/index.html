01 / THE RULESv1 · review before trading

A simple monthly discipline.

  • Start with Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Tesla.
  • Measure each stock’s 3-, 6-, and 12-month returns at the prior month-end.
  • Rank each period from 1 (best) to 7 (worst). Buy the stock with the highest average rank.
  • Start with a $50,000 reallocation; add $15,000 in fresh funds each month.
  • Monthly position handling is awaiting strategy review.
  • No stop-loss rule is currently configured. Review monthly; no automatic mid-month exits.
  • Review funding and concentration before trading. Record confirmed fills separately from proposals.

“Hated” means weak price performance here—not negative headlines, sentiment, or a forecast of recovery.

Methodology, data provenance & limitations +

Signal construction

Inspired by Sheel Mohnot’s original post. Our funding plan adds $15,000 monthly; his pictured example uses a fixed $100,000. The screenshot specifies rotation, but his sell-timing reply was undecided. Our monthly rotation rule is an explicit choice; no stop-loss rule was verified.

Use the last NYSE trading session on or before the previous calendar month-end, and the month-ends 3, 6, and 12 months earlier. Return = adjusted closing price ÷ starting adjusted closing price − 1.

Exact return ties receive their average rank. Average-rank ties go to the worst 12-month return, then 6-month, then 3-month, then ticker alphabetically.

Data & audit trail

Yahoo Finance adjusted daily prices are a total-return proxy. The original post does not specify dividend treatment; our explicit convention may differ from its backtest. This is an unofficial data endpoint; missing or stale observations block new signals. Store original provider responses, retrieval times, source URLs, and the exact price observations in Supabase.

Execution & risk

The bot prepares research and allocations. It cannot submit brokerage orders. Funding must remain inside the account being reinvested; IRA withdrawals are not part of the strategy.

A single-company purchase can lose substantially and keep underperforming. The strategy rotates its invested balance into one company. Monthly additions increase that concentration. No maximum strategy allocation has been chosen yet.
